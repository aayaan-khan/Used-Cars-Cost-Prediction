# Decision log

- D1: Primary dataset is CarDekho Car details v3 (Indian market, has owner and seller_type).
- D2: UK audi.csv is only an optional secondary generalisation check, never mixed into training.
- D3: De-duplicate before any train/test split (1,202 exact duplicate rows found).
- D4: "torque" is a candidate to drop (inconsistent units).
- D5: units parsed to numeric; "torque" dropped (inconsistent formats).
- D6: age = 2020 - year, because the data ends in 2020.
- D7: custom model_key (218 groups) replaces the naive two-word key.
- D8: outlier rule is implied km per year > 100,000 (4 rows removed); high-priced cars are kept.
- D9: 87 CNG/LPG rows report mileage in km/kg, so fuel stays in the model.
- D10: the 209 rows with missing specs are informative (older, cheaper); no dropna. Impute within model and fuel inside CV folds, plus a missing flag.
- D11: target is log(price): skew 5.6 -> -0.16, and depreciation is multiplicative.
- D12: raw brand and mileage comparisons are confounded; report within-model estimates (age -9.8%/yr, 10k km -0.9%, diesel +13.6%, automatic +11.3%, non-first owner -7.7%).
- D13: final test set is 20% of car models, locked until the last step.
- D14: development CV is StratifiedGroupKFold (groups = model, stratified by price band).
- D15: select models on grouped CV, and also report the random-split (seen-model) number.
- D16: baselines B0 median, B1 median by age, B2 age + log km; bar to clear is MedAPE about 29%.
- D17: leakage demo: R2 0.934 (duplicates, random) -> 0.906 (de-duplicated, random) -> 0.810 (grouped by model).
- D18: features exclude model identity; model_key is only the grouping key for validation.
- D19: all preprocessing (median imputation with missing-indicators, scaling, one-hot) sits inside the pipeline.
- D20: every model is scored in two regimes: grouped by model (stress test) and random on de-duplicated data (typical repeat listing).
- D21: model-aware imputation tested and rejected (no consistent gain); this revises D10 to plain median imputation plus indicators.
- D22: selection rule = one-standard-deviation band on grouped RMSE_log, then best typical-listing MedAPE -> LightGBM (15 leaves, 500 trees, lr 0.03). Final test set untouched.
- D23: prediction intervals use Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) at nominal 90% (alpha=0.10, 5th to 95th percentiles) on log(price) to guarantee distribution-free finite-sample coverage.
- D24: base quantiles use LightGBM quantile regression (15 leaves, 500 trees, lr 0.03) vs linear QuantileRegressor baseline; linear quantiles over-inflate to 222% width, while LightGBM with CQR delivers competitive 64-87% widths and lower Winkler score.
- D25: calibration sets are held out strictly by model_key inside training folds (nested 4:1 split) without touching the locked test set.
- D26: slice diagnostics on unseen models under standard LightGBM_CQR measure 80.8% coverage in true Q1 and 83.5% in true Q5 (vs 93%+ in middle tiers); when sliced by predicted price quintile, coverage is 88.2% (P1), 87.2% (P2), 83.5% (P3), 92.0% (P4), and 90.5% (P5).
- D27: Mondrian bins are constructed on predicted log-mid-price from the LightGBM quantile base models on the calibration fold; no true price labels are used at fit or inference time, making this variant fully deployable without ground-truth leakage.
- D28: LightGBM_CQR 5-fold stability measures mean coverage 88.27% +/- 5.13% (grouped) and 89.53% +/- 1.42% (seen); median width measures 86.30% +/- 5.96% (grouped) and 63.98% +/- 2.75% (seen).
- D29: calibration split sensitivity across 5 random seeds (0, 7, 21, 42, 99) measures global Q_adj range of 0.0169 (min 0.0877, max 0.1046, default seed 42 = 0.0911).
- D30: fuel slice breakdown by age and predicted price measures lower coverage for diesel in older buckets (80.7% in 8-12 yrs, 79.1% in 13+ yrs vs petrol 91.5% and 87.2%) and lower predicted price quintiles (78.9% in P1 vs petrol 90.5%); reported without causal claims.
- D31: Section 6c evaluated three candidates to address diesel under-coverage (A: LightGBM_CQR, B: Fuel-conditional CQR with pooled CNG/LPG, C: Locally Adaptive CQR normalised by predicted difficulty sigma(x)). Programmatic decision rule evaluated worst-segment coverage (Diesel/Petrol x predicted-price quintile) subject to grouped coverage >= 88% and median width <= 1.10 x Candidate A (<= 95.59%). Candidate C achieved worst-segment coverage 79.1% (P3 Diesel) vs 78.9% for Candidates A (P1 Diesel) and B (P3 Diesel), with overall grouped coverage 88.17% (seen 89.71%) and median width 85.7% (seen 64.8%), winning selection as C_Adaptive_CQR. Seed stability across 5 inner calibration seeds in full outer 5-fold CV measured out-of-fold grouped coverage range of 1.05% (88.17% to 89.22%), median width range of 2.0% (85.7% to 87.7%), and mean calibration adjustment range of 0.010 (0.157 to 0.167). Candidate C artifact saved to models/c_adaptive_cqr_model.joblib.
- D32: the pre-committed rule had no minimum-improvement margin, so a 0.2-point difference decided the selection; any future pre-registered rule should include one. No candidate closed the diesel coverage gap.
- D33: Section 6 is frozen; downstream sections use candidate C intervals with the diesel under-coverage disclosed.
- D34: near-duplicate audit (Section 8). 169 twin groups / 351 rows (6.3% of development rows) share name, year, km, fuel, seller_type, transmission and owner but have different prices (median spread 11.6%, 75th percentile 23.3%). With twin groups kept in one fold (GroupKFold, 5 folds, seed 1) the repeat-listing median error moves by -0.22 to +0.04 points across the six models and CQR coverage in that regime moves 89.5% to 90.2% (standard) and 89.7% to 90.5% (adaptive): twins do not inflate the repeat-listing results. Grouped-by-model results are unaffected by construction (asserted in Cell 36). The D22 rule would now pick HistGradientBoosting (11.85% vs 11.90%, a 0.05-point gap); LightGBM is retained because the intervals are built on LightGBM quantile regression and for SHAP support. This is the D32 weakness again: no minimum-improvement margin.
- D35: the CQR classes (MondrianCQR, FuelMondrianCQR, LocallyAdaptiveCQR) and compute_cqr_adjustment moved from the notebook to src/conformal.py, because a class defined in a notebook is pickled as __main__.<Class> and cannot be loaded elsewhere. LocallyAdaptiveCQR now takes its sigma(x) pipeline as diff_model (the notebook's DIFF_MODEL). Sections 1-6 outputs are identical to a fresh run of the previous notebook except one print line.
- D36: final point model = LightGBM (D22) fitted on all development rows, saved to models/lightgbm_point_model.joblib with its feature list and library versions; src/deal_score.py loads it together with the interval artifact (C_Adaptive_CQR). The point model uses all development rows while the interval models use 75% of development models (the rest is the calibration set). Models are git-ignored; the README explains how to regenerate them.
- D37: error analysis (Section 9) on development out-of-fold predictions only; no model change. Errors concentrate in whole car models that are mispriced the same way when held out (median signed error: Tata Nano +130%, Mahindra Xylo +78%, Tata Indica +42%, Maruti 800 +39%, Mahindra KUV +37%, Chevrolet Beat +33%, Toyota Innova -40%; 8.8% of development rows), and the worst 10% of rows give 29% of the total log error. Typical error rises with age (12.5% for 0-3 years to 29.9% for 13+) and is worse with missing specs (25.5% vs 17.3%). No systematic bias by predicted price (-6% to -1%). The fair price is >=30% above the listing for 14.3% of unseen-model listings (9.2% of repeat listings) and >=30% below for 11.2% (4.6%), so the Deal Score needs a confidence signal for rarely seen models, old cars and missing specs. Diesel and petrol point errors are equal (17.7% vs 17.5%); without the seven problem models the share of listings off by >30% is 21.2% for diesel and 21.7% for petrol, a hypothesis for the D30 gap (the model list was chosen after looking, so exploratory). Possible data errors flagged, not removed: a 2020 VW Polo 1.5 TDI at Rs 2.6 lakh and a 2016 VW GTI at Rs 8 lakh. Untested: model identity as a feature, to be judged on repeat listings only.
- D38: car-model identity as a feature (Section 10). Rule fixed before any result: a familiarity-routed variant (identity model only for car models with >=10 listings in the training fold, otherwise the baseline) is adopted only if it lowers repeat-listing MedAPE by >=0.5 points, lowers RMSE_log and wins >=4 of 5 paired folds (twin-grouped folds, same hyper-parameters, no re-tuning). Result: one-hot identity, routed, 11.90% -> 11.35% MedAPE (gain 0.55, RMSE_log 0.222 -> 0.218, 5/5 folds; bootstrap over car models 95% interval 0.25 to 0.78 points; any k from 1 to 50 gives 11.35-11.46%), so it passes the rule, but by 0.05 points (the D32 weakness). Target-encoded identity fails (0.38 points) and, applied to all rows, worsens unseen models from 17.6% to 20.1%. Routing leaves unseen-model predictions identical to the baseline. Gains come from car models with >=20 listings; models with <20 listings do not move. No saved artifact changed: adopting identity means refitting the point model and recalibrating the Section 6 intervals on the new features, pending the project owner's decision. The selection folds are also the reporting folds, so the locked test set remains the honest check.
- D39: SHAP explanations (Section 11, src/explain.py), no model change. SHAP values of the saved LightGBM point model are added up per listing feature (52 encoded columns -> 12 drivers) and are exact on the log scale (asserted to 1e-9), so fair price = average price x product of factors. Age 39% and power 22% of mean |SHAP|; fuel, brand, engine 8% each. On the five held-out car-model folds the ranking holds (rank correlation min 0.92, median 0.97) and agrees with permutation importance (0.94). Rated mileage and seats have no held-out signal (shuffling does not raise the error in 4 of 5 folds); kept for now, removal would need re-validation. Credit is shared among correlated features (kilometres is 5% because age carries it), so effects are descriptions of the model, not causal. deal_score gained a shared _as_frame helper; its outputs are unchanged.

Reproduction check: coverage 88.17% / 90.45% and median width 85.7% / 65.2% (unseen / repeat). The validation labelling functions agree with src/deal_score.py on 450 sampled calls. Rules were fixed in 12.1 before any result; the operating curve in 12.5 was added afterwards and changes no rule. The locked test set and all saved models are untouched.
- D41: injection test. A +20% markup is flagged Overpriced for 20.6% (unseen) and 26.9% (repeat) of listings, +30% for 30.8% and 41.1%, +50% for 50.4% and 63.5%; a -20% cut is flagged Underpriced for 21.6% and 29.8%, -30% for 39.4% and 54.3%. Untouched listings are flagged either way 11.8% (unseen) and 9.5% (repeat), an upper bound on false alarms. Smallest tested change detected at least half the time (cut / markup): unseen x0.60 / x1.50, repeat x0.70 / x1.50; at least 80%: unseen not reached / not reached, repeat x0.50 / x2.00. The Deal Score therefore detects large price gaps only. The median spread between listings of the same car is 11.6% (Section 8), so part of the range width may be asking-price noise; not tested.

- D45: open point for Step 12. The repeat-regime intervals above are calibrated on random (twin-grouped) calibration rows, but the shipped artifact models/c_adaptive_cqr_model.joblib is calibrated on held-out car models (Section 6c, Cell 33), so for a listing of a well-known car model the app behaves more like the unseen-models column (median width 86% of price) than the repeat column (65%). Calibrated on repeat listings the intervals cover only 85.9% of rare-model and 71.8% of unknown-model listings (n=377 and 39); a possible reason is that such models behave like unseen ones, not tested. Step 12 should decide whether to calibrate known and rare/unknown models separately, then re-run Section 12: src/deal_validation.py works on out-of-fold arrays, so the same cells apply to the new point model and intervals. The demo app (Step 14) needs dev.model_key.value_counts() and the entered car model to raise the rare/unknown-model warning.

Results on the repeat-listing folds: known models 90.6% coverage (shipped 93.7%) at median width 62.0% of price (shipped 75.3%, 18% narrower); rare + unknown 90.1% coverage with the shipped range (84.6% with random calibration); detection x1.20 20.0% to 27.8%, x0.80 21.0% to 33.0%; untouched known-model listings flagged 6.3% to 9.4%. All four rules pass; routing is adopted. Overall: median error 11.90% to 11.35%, RMSE_log 0.222 to 0.218, coverage 93.4% to 90.5%, width 76.6% to 64.1%, interval score 1.044 to 0.960. Resolves D45 and implements D38 (identity is used for known car models only).

- D49: pre-registration of the final test (Section 14), committed before the test was run. One run, on the shipped artifacts as saved; no tuning, re-fitting or re-choosing afterwards; failed criteria are reported, not repaired; the models do not change after the test. The test set holds only car models never seen in development, so it measures the general route and cannot test the known-model route of D46. Primary criteria: T1a/T1b test MedAPE and RMSE_log at most the development CV mean + 2 fold SDs; T1c beats baselines B1 and B2; T2a range coverage 85-93%; T2b median width within +/-20% of development. Secondary: T3 detection of x1.20 and x0.80 within 5 points of development and untouched flags at most 15%; T4 error ratio flagged/unflagged at least 1.3 for old car, specs missing and either (not evaluable below 30 flagged listings); T5 SHAP driver ranking test vs development Spearman at least 0.8. Before the real run the cells were exercised once on a development sample with a fake car model (a dry run that touches no test data) to catch code errors.

MedAPE 19.06% (limit 19.83%), RMSE_log 0.288 (limit 0.371), best baseline MedAPE 34.24%: T1a-c PASS. Range coverage 84.12% (floor 85%): T2a FAIL; width 80.6% (68.6 to 102.8): T2b PASS.
